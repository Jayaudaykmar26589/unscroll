META GLOBAL AI DEVELOPER HACKATHON · PUSH THE FRONTIER

A test for the tracing failure the Vesuvius Challenge sees most: a trace switching sheets. Built here, and used to double how often the AI stays on the sheet you clicked.

The Herculaneum scrolls were carbonized by Vesuvius in 79 AD and cannot be opened. They are read from CT scans by following one papyrus sheet through thousands of slices, where neighbouring sheets are crushed together. If a trace slips onto the neighbouring sheet, everything after it reads the wrong page, and nothing warns you. The Vesuvius Challenge says no method yet traces a complete, correct surface automatically, and lists sheet switches among the failures it sees most. Unscroll turns one click into a SAM 3.1 trace, measures it against sheets people traced by hand, and fixes the slice where traces go wrong. It has not read any new text; it measures how far from reading a trace is.

Words used here. Sheet: one layer of rolled papyrus. Slice: one CT image across the scroll. Trace: the line the AI draws along one sheet, slice after slice. Click: where a person points at a sheet to start a trace. 40 µm is 5 voxels (image pixels) of this scan, which has 7.91 µm voxels.

VESUVIUS CHALLENGE OPEN PROBLEMS THIS ADDRESSES

#2 · Meshing and tracing

The Challenge says: traces switching onto a neighbouring sheet are a main failure; one small error sends a trace onto the wrong sheet.

Unscroll: measures those switches against sheets people traced, on 89 clicks with classic baselines; the first-slice fix reduces them.

Vesuvius Challenge open problems ↗

#5 · Label quality

The Challenge says: hand-made traces drift and jump sheets; ask people to fix only the uncertain parts.

Unscroll: ranks traces riskiest-first without a reference; on held-out clicks, checking 73% of traces catches every wrong-sheet one.

Vesuvius Challenge open problems ↗

#6 · Ink diagnostics

The Challenge says: better diagnostics are needed to tell “no ink” from “ink not yet recovered”.

Unscroll: a steady error under 40 µm keeps the letters; wobble from slice to slice of 16–40 µm destroys them.
