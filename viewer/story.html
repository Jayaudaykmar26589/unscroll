WHAT UNSCROLL IS, IN FIVE THINGS YOU CAN TOUCH

How do you read a book that turns to dust if you open it?

In 79 AD, Mount Vesuvius buried a Roman library. Its scrolls are still there, burnt black and solid as charcoal. Unroll one and it crumbles. So people scan them instead, and try to read the inside without ever opening them. Unscroll is one small piece of that job. Scroll down and try each step yourself.

Start ↓

1 · SQUASH A SCROLL

A scroll is one long sheet, rolled up.

Picture a poster rolled into a tube: one sheet, going round and round. Cut across it and you see a spiral. The writing is on the inside of that spiral.

After the eruption the scrolls were carbonised and crushed. The spiral didn't stay neat. The layers got pressed together, bent and folded, with no air between them.

DRAG THE SLIDER, PRESS "SHOW THE REAL SCAN", THEN DRAG IT AGAIN TO ZOOM IN

The real picture is a CT scan: an X-ray slice straight across the scroll. It is 1 of more than 14,000 slices, stacked like a loaf of bread. Every bright curve is one layer of papyrus. In this project a layer is called a sheet.

A fresh scroll: one sheet, wound round with a little space between the turns.

2 · FOLLOW ONE SHEET

Pick a sheet. Now follow it through the next 50 slices.

To read one page you have to follow the same sheet from slice to slice, through thousands of slices. The sheets are packed so tight that the one you are following can look exactly like its neighbour.

Unscroll does this with a click. You point at a sheet on one slice. SAM 3.1, Meta's video-tracking model, follows it through the stack as if the slices were frames of a video. Unscroll's own trick is on the first slice: it walks along the bright ridge of the sheet instead of trusting a rough outline, so the tracking starts in the right place.

3 · HALF A HAIR

Why 40 micrometres? Because the ink is invisible.

The ink on these scrolls is carbon, like the papyrus around it. It doesn't show up in the scan. In 2023 a team won the Vesuvius Challenge Grand Prize with an AI that spots the ink's faint texture, but it needs to be told exactly where the sheet is. A human hair is about 70 µm wide. The trace must stay within about half a hair of the sheet.

Unscroll measured how far off a trace can be before the letters vanish. It took the known sheet, pushed its trace off by a set amount, and asked the ink model to read it. Two kinds of error: a steady offset (the whole trace shifted the same way) and a wobble (jumping about from slice to slice).

SLIDE THE ERROR. SWITCH STEADY TO WOBBLY.

A steady error of 5 voxels still keeps most of the letters. A wobble of 5 voxels wipes them out. That is the finding that turned the project towards making traces smooth, not just close.

4 · THE TEST

Does the fix work on clicks it has never seen?

It is easy to make a tracer look good on the examples you built it with. So Unscroll was tested on 74 fresh clicks, placed on sheets people had traced by hand, that it never saw while being built. Each click was traced through 20 slices, before and after the first-slice fix, and compared with the hand-traced sheet.

The score is simple: how much of the trace stays within 40 µm of the sheet. Every dot on the right is one click.

PRESS "RUN THE TEST"

Before the fix, half of a typical trace stayed on the sheet only a quarter of the time. After, half. That is a real gain, measured on new clicks, and it is also honest about what is left: half of each trace is still more than 40 µm off. A classic computer-vision tracer (optical flow), given the same fixed first slice, does nearly as well; Unscroll beats it by a few points and, unlike it, stops when it loses the sheet.

5 · TRUE OR FALSE

What Unscroll is, and what it isn't.

Projects like this are easy to oversell. These four cards say exactly where it stands. Click each one.

CLICK A CARD TO FLIP IT

The short version: Unscroll is a measuring tool for sheet-tracing. It turns a click into a trace, tells you how far that trace drifted from a sheet people traced by hand, ranks traces by risk, and shows how steady a trace must be before ink can be read from it. It has not read a single new letter. Yet.
